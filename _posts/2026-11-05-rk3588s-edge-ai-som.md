# RK3588S Rockchip SoM: Powerful 8nm Module for Industrial Edge AI and Embedded Vision

![RK3588S Rockchip SoM for edge computing and embedded AI]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/rk3588s-embedded-som-module.webp)

If you’re building something in embedded AI, multi-screen displays, or machine vision, the core module you pick makes a huge difference. That’s why so many developers go for the Rockchip RK3588S in industrial edge computing—it’s easy to see the appeal.

This chip uses an 8nm process, so it delivers strong performance while running cool. Inside, you get a hybrid setup: four Cortex-A76 cores for the heavy-duty stuff, plus four Cortex-A55 cores that quietly handle background work while sipping less power. The A76 cluster can hit up to 2.4GHz, so you’ve got plenty of muscle without the risk of overheating.

But the real star here is the on-board NPU. If AI matters to your project—object detection, face recognition, image classification, video analysis—this module has your back. The NPU tackles these jobs right on the device, so you don’t have to rely on the cloud or stable internet just to get results.

For graphics and displays, the RK3588S can handle 8K video decoding and power several screens at once. It shows up everywhere: industrial control panels, eye-catching digital signage, NVRs, and smart display systems.

You’ve got options with both Linux and Android. RAM? That’s up to you—choose anything from 1GB to 32GB of LPDDR4 or LPDDR4X. Storage flexes too, from 4GB to 128GB of eMMC. So it’s easy to match your hardware to your budget, with no need to cut corners.

Since it’s a system-on-module, all the tricky processor design is already handled and tested. All you need is a custom carrier board with your choice of power, I/O, and peripherals. That means less risk and much faster development.

Where does the RK3588S really stand out? Here’s where people use it:
- Edge AI gateways in industrial settings
- Computer vision cameras
- Industrial HMIs and control panels
- Multiple digital displays in one setup
- Standalone AI recognition tools
- Powerful IoT controllers

FAQ

Q1: How does the RK3588S differ from the RK3588?
A: The S version is simply more budget-friendly. You keep most of the processor, NPU, and media power, but it drops a few of the premium connections. If you don’t need all the high-speed extras, RK3588S keeps things affordable.

Q2: Can I run my own AI models on its NPU?
A: Yep! Just convert your model to the RKNN format. The official toolkit covers PyTorch, ONNX, and similar frameworks for local inference.

Q3: Is the RK3588S good for making lots of units?
A: Absolutely. The core circuits are solid and proven, with reliable BSPs for both Linux and Android.

Q4: What about the carrier board?
A: You’ll need to design a custom one for your project—with power, Ethernet, USB, CSI, HDMI, whatever you need. Just stick to the module’s pin specs.

Q5: Does it come with WiFi and Bluetooth?
A: Not built-in. You’ll add external WiFi/Bluetooth modules on your baseboard.
